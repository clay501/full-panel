## Email 2: Where your next client is probably looking

**Send:** day 2
**Subject:** Where your next client is probably looking
**Preview text:** It isn't Instagram.

Hi {{first_name}},

When people decide they're ready for therapy, more of them turn to their primary care provider than to anywhere else. In Thriveworks' 2025 survey of more than 2,000 U.S. adults, 39% said they'd look to their doctor first, ahead of friends and family, insurance company resources, search engines and directories.

Most therapists I talk to have a Psychology Today profile and no relationship with a single physician. That's the opportunity.

Before I trained as a counselor, building referral relationships was my job, at a primary care company and at home health and hospice agencies. The doctors who referred the most weren't the ones I charmed. They were the ones who knew exactly who to send me, heard back when a patient connected, and trusted me to follow through.

If you want to try it this week, here's a short version of the email I'd send to a nearby practice:

> Hi Dr. [Name],
>
> I'm [Your name], a [credential] in [town], and I work with [specific population and concern]. I imagine you see patients who [specific situation], and I'd like to be a reliable place to send them.
>
> I have openings now and reach out to referred patients within one business day. I [take X and Y insurance / charge $___ and provide superbills]. With your patient's written permission, I'll let your office know they've connected with me.
>
> Would a short call with you or your care team be helpful?

The full guide, with the reasoning behind each line and how to close the loop with a client's consent, is here: [How to get referrals from doctors as a therapist](https://fullpanel.co/blog/therapist-referrals-from-doctors/).

Thanks,
Clay

## Email 3: Two fixes that take an afternoon

**Send:** day 5
**Subject:** Two fixes that take an afternoon
**Preview text:** Most people decide in the first sentence.

Hi {{first_name}},

Referral relationships take months to build. These two fixes take an afternoon, and they make every other kind of marketing work better.

**1. Rewrite the first sentence of your Psychology Today profile.** The opening lines are what people see in search results before they click. If yours starts with your license or years of experience, swap it for a sentence about what your ideal client is going through, in their words. "You're holding it together at work and falling apart at home" will get more clicks than "I'm an LPC with 10 years of experience."

**2. Say what it costs.** In the same Thriveworks survey, 95% of people said knowing their out-of-pocket cost before booking was important, and 39% only book when they know it in advance. Put your fee, the insurance you take, and a one-line explanation of superbills on your website and your profile. It feels uncomfortable for a lot of therapists, and it's one of the easiest ways to get more people to reach out.

If you'd like the whole list, here's the [Psychology Today profile checklist](https://fullpanel.co/blog/psychology-today-profile-checklist/).

Thanks,
Clay
