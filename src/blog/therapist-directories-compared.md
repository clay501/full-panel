You don't need to be everywhere. Most solo practices do best with one paid directory they keep sharp, one or two free or values-aligned listings, and accurate insurance directories if they're in network. The trick is knowing what you're actually paying for, because "directory" now covers three very different business models.

## Three kinds of listing

- **Subscription directories.** You pay a flat monthly fee for a profile. Psychology Today, Zencare, GoodTherapy, TherapyDen's paid tier and Inclusive Therapists work this way. You keep your fee and your clients.
- **Insurance platforms.** Headway, Alma, Grow Therapy and Rula handle credentialing and billing, and list you in a client-facing search. Some are free to join and take a margin on each session; others charge a membership.
- **Pay-per-booking marketplaces.** Zocdoc charges you when a new patient books, so your cost rises with your success.

And don't forget the free one that matters most if you take insurance: **your listing in each insurance company's own directory.** In Thriveworks' [2025 survey](https://thriveworks.com/help-with/research/therapy-seeking-decisions-2025/), 31% of people said they'd look to their insurer's resources when ready for therapy, more than the 26% who'd use an online directory. Those listings are often wrong. Check your address, specialties and "accepting new patients" status with every plan you're in.

## How to choose

1. **Start with where your clients look.** If you're in network, fix your insurance directory listings first; they're free. If you're private pay, Psychology Today is still the baseline.
2. **Add one listing that matches your niche.** If you center LGBTQ+, BIPOC or neurodivergent clients, a values-aligned directory like Inclusive Therapists often brings better-fit inquiries than a bigger one.
3. **Be careful stacking insurance platforms.** Joining Headway, Alma and Grow at once can mean different rates, contracts and rules for the same payer. Read the contract terms, especially what happens to your clients and contracts if you leave.
4. **Check licensure requirements.** Several directories require full, independent licensure. If you're pre-licensed, confirm what each one allows and how they display your supervisor.
5. **Track results for 90 days.** Ask every new client how they found you. A listing that hasn't produced a single inquiry in three months isn't worth keeping.

## My short version

For a new solo private-pay practice: a sharp Psychology Today profile, one free or low-cost niche directory, and a well-kept Google Business Profile. For an in-network practice: accurate payer directories first, then Psychology Today, then decide whether an insurance platform's convenience is worth its cut.
